## OctoAcme Project Management Summary

OctoAcme follows a structured, lifecycle-based approach to project management grounded in five core principles: customer-first value delivery, iterative development, clear ownership, data-informed decisions, and psychological safety. The organization operates through a well-defined lifecycle consisting of five phases: **Initiation** (validating business need and stakeholder alignment via a Project One-pager), **Planning** (breaking work into shippable increments with prioritized backlogs and risk registers), **Execution** (day-to-day delivery with daily standups and weekly syncs), **Release** (standardized deployment with pre-release checklists and rollback plans), and **Close & Retrospective** (capturing learnings and driving continuous improvement). This end-to-end structure ensures that projects move through clear decision gates and maintain transparency at each stage, with success metrics and acceptance criteria defined upfront and tracked throughout delivery.

The organization assigns clear ownership through three primary roles: **Project Managers** who coordinate schedules, manage risks, and facilitate communication; **Product Managers** who define outcomes, prioritize backlogs, and measure impact; and **Developers** (supported by QA/Testing and Stakeholders) who implement features while maintaining quality standards. This role clarity prevents ambiguity and ensures accountability across cross-functional teams. Communication happens through a consistent cadence of weekly PM-PdM syncs, twice-weekly delivery standups, and monthly stakeholder updates, with ad-hoc escalations following a clear three-level escalation path (team-level triage → PM to Product Lead → Sponsor-level escalation for business-impacting issues).

Quality and execution are maintained through disciplined workflows and comprehensive checklists. The team uses GitHub Projects with standardized columns (Backlog, Ready, In Progress, In Review, QA, Done), enforces small PRs (≤400 lines when possible), requires CI passing and at least one approval before merge, and implements unit, integration, and end-to-end testing. Risk management is continuous—risks are identified during planning, assessed for impact and likelihood, captured in a Risk Register, and reviewed weekly during syncs. Release management follows a defined pre-release checklist covering acceptance criteria, CI/security scans, release notes, and smoke tests, with documented rollback procedures and incident playbooks to minimize production risk.

## Core Principles

- **Customer-first**: Prioritize customer value and usability
- **Iterative delivery**: Ship small, testable increments
- **Clear ownership**: Each project has a PM and Product Lead
- **Data-informed**: Measure impact and iterate on evidence
- **Psychological safety**: Encourage feedback and learning

## Key Artifacts

- Project Charter / One-pager
- Roadmap and Release Plan
- Sprint/Iteration Backlog with Acceptance Criteria
